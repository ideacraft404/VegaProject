01 · The gap that exists today in deep-tech hardware sectors

A prototype proves the technology works. It doesn't prove there's a business.

After technical proof, everything has to work together: the design, the people, the suppliers, the factory, the certification, the capital and the customer. In India today, the support for each of these is thin, scattered or hard to reach.

BUILD IT
The expertise is scarce.

Few early hardware teams have reliability, test and design-for-manufacture skills in-house, and few engineers in India have done it at scale. Design choices made now lock in the unit economics.

MAKE IT
Suppliers rarely bet on low volume.

Few suppliers and manufacturers will back an evolving design at small volumes. Testing, tooling and certification capacity is scattered, and hard to book in time.

SELL IT
Pilots rarely become orders.

Institutional buyers run proof of concept after pilot after pilot. Few startups have someone beside them who has turned pilots into purchase orders before.

All three have to come together before the runway runs out. The technology keeps progressing. The business runs out of time.

05 · Why a layer like VEGA matters in the country

Every country that became a hardware nation built a bridge from lab to market.

Some built it through the state, others through private and university institutions.
India has funded the prototypes, and the bridge to market is still being joined up.

GOVERNMENT-LED
Taiwan

ITRI, a public research institute set up in 1973, turned lab work into companies. It spun out UMC & then TSMC, & anchored the Hsinchu Science Park, where engineering, fabs, suppliers & capital sit side by side, within a short drive of each other.

Result: a world leader in advanced chipmaking.

GOVERNMENT-LED
Israel

The Israel Innovation Authority funds commercialisation with grants repaid as royalties only when products sell. Its Yozma programme in the 1990s co-invested with private funds, building the venture capital industry that backs hard tech today.

Result: a startup nation far bigger than its size.

PRIVATE & UNIVERSITY-LED
United States

Institutions like The Engine, founded by MIT, and HAX give hard-tech companies labs, engineers and patient capital. Federal programmes such as SBIR and DARPA fund early technology, and agencies like the Department of Defense act as first buyers.

Result: a steady path from university lab to startup.

IN THE MAKING
India

DLI, PLI, iDEX and the Semiconductor Mission are funding prototypes and building parts of the support layer. What's still forming is one path that joins engineering, manufacturing, certification, capital and buyers, to build India as a Hardware Product Nation.

VEGA is designed to play a critical role here.
